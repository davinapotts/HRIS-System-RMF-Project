# Risk Scoring Methodology

The HRIS risk register uses a qualitative risk scoring approach to evaluate and prioritize identified risks. Risk levels are determined based on two primary factors: likelihood and impact.

This methodology helps support consistent risk evaluation and assists stakeholders in prioritizing mitigation efforts.

---

## Likelihood Scale

Likelihood represents the probability that a threat event may occur.

| Score | Likelihood | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Rare | Very unlikely to occur |
| 2 | Unlikely | Possible but not expected to occur frequently |
| 3 | Possible | May occur occasionally under certain conditions |
| 4 | Likely | Expected to occur periodically |
| 5 | Almost Certain | Expected to occur frequently |

---

## Impact Scale

Impact represents the potential effect on the organization if the risk event occurs.

| Score | Impact | Description |
|------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Negligible | Minimal operational disruption |
| 2 | Low | Limited operational or business impact |
| 3 | Moderate | Noticeable disruption to operations or services |
| 4 | High | Significant disruption to HR operations or data security |
| 5 | Severe | Critical system failure, regulatory impact, or major data breach |

---

## Risk Score Calculation

Risk score is determined by multiplying likelihood and impact values.

Example:

Likelihood = 3  
Impact = 4  

Risk Score = 12

---

## Risk Level Determination

| Risk Score | Risk Level |
|-----------|-----------|
| 1–4 | Low |
| 5–9 | Medium |
| 10–15 | High |
| 16–25 | Critical |

---

## Application to the HRIS Risk Register

The risks identified in this project were evaluated using qualitative analysis of threat likelihood and potential business impact. Risk treatment decisions such as mitigation or risk transfer were selected based on the resulting risk level and the effectiveness of existing security controls.
